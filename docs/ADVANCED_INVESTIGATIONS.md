# Advanced Investigations — Locked

These modules are lower priority than primary money/product/conflict work, but they become important when evidence points to them.

## 1. Astroturf / fake-engagement audit

Look for:
- suspicious follower growth;
- bot-like repost networks;
- identical comments;
- coordinated posting windows;
- purchased likes/views;
- engagement pods;
- repeated anonymous accounts amplifying the same claim.

Do not label activity inauthentic without platform evidence or strong behavioral indicators.

## 2. Sockpuppet / undisclosed identity audit

Investigate whether apparently independent accounts, reviewers, experts, or commenters are actually controlled by:
- the seller;
- employee;
- family member;
- affiliate;
- PR contractor;
- connected organization.

Use public evidence only.

## 3. Reputation-management / SEO flooding

Look for:
- paid PR placements;
- large numbers of near-identical profile articles;
- reputation-management firms;
- search-result flooding;
- sponsored "expert" profiles;
- removal/suppression campaigns;
- coordinated positive-review generation.

## 4. Review manipulation

Test:
- review bursts;
- copied wording;
- same reviewer across related products;
- incentivized reviews;
- employee/affiliate reviews;
- review-gating;
- removal of negative feedback.

## 5. Data / privacy monetization

Audit:
- privacy policies;
- mailing-list sharing;
- data brokers;
- retargeting pixels;
- cross-site tracking;
- customer-data sharing;
- list rental;
- partner marketing.

Question:
**Does the audience itself become a monetized product?**

## 6. Cross-border entity / tax-jurisdiction mapping

Where material, trace:
- foreign companies;
- offshore entities;
- trusts;
- foundations;
- licensing entities;
- payment routing;
- tax domiciles.

A foreign/offshore structure is not wrongdoing by itself.

## 7. Crowdfunding / donation flows

Track:
- crowdfunding campaigns;
- donation appeals;
- legal-defense funds;
- emergency campaigns;
- nonprofit fundraising;
- recurring donor programs.

Compare stated purpose with public financial evidence where available.

## 8. Legal intimidation / content suppression

Document verified:
- cease-and-desist letters;
- defamation lawsuits;
- DMCA takedowns;
- platform complaints;
- threats against critics;
- settlement confidentiality.

Do not interpret legitimate legal defense as intimidation without evidence.

## 9. Platform migration

Track when a person/product moves among:
YouTube → Rumble → Odysee → Substack → Telegram → private membership.

This can preserve deleted or revised claims and reveal audience-transfer funnels.

## 10. Credibility laundering

Test whether credibility is manufactured through:
- circular guest introductions;
- repeated "expert" labels;
- self-awarded titles;
- think tanks controlled by insiders;
- conferences that repeatedly credential the same people;
- paid awards;
- sponsored academic-looking reports.

Map who confers credibility on whom.

## 11. Media citation echo

Track when:
one claim → podcast → blog → newsletter → interview → later cited as "many reports."

Collapse the echo chain back to its original evidence.

## 12. Audience-value analysis

Where possible estimate:
- email list size;
- subscriber count;
- conversion points;
- affiliate value;
- sponsorship inventory;
- customer lifetime value.

Purpose:
understand why certain narratives may be economically valuable to repeat.

## Publication rule

These modules are supporting evidence. None should substitute for primary proof of fraud, manipulation, or coordinated misconduct.
