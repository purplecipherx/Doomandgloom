# Research Standard

## 1. Minimum source count

Each investigated person, company, organization, platform, or product line receives **at least 50 distinct sources** before a final profile is considered complete.

A URL count is not a source count. Mirrors, copied press releases, syndication, quote-farms, and articles that merely repeat the same originating report count as one evidentiary lineage.

## 2. Preferred source hierarchy

Use the strongest available source for each proposition.

1. Court opinions, indictments, plea agreements, sentencing records
2. Government / regulator records (SEC, CFTC, DOJ, FTC, FDA, state registries, tax/nonprofit filings, parliamentary records, central banks)
3. Corporate filings, registries, contracts, patents, trademark records
4. Original scientific papers / technical documentation
5. Original dated statements, videos, newsletters, marketing pages, product pages
6. Archived copies of original material
7. Major investigative journalism
8. Specialist journalism / named expert analysis
9. Customer reviews and complaint sites
10. Reddit, forums, social posts, comments

Items 9-10 are lead generators and evidence of reported customer experience, **not proof of the underlying allegation by themselves**.

## 3. Adversarial claim testing

For every important claim:

**What they said → source they relied on → what that source actually says → added inference → independent corroboration → assessment**

Never substitute "this sounds ridiculous" for evidence.

## 4. Relationship classes

Every edge in the network graph must use one of these relationship classes:

- **FINANCIAL** — payment, ownership, investment, commission, affiliate/referral, sponsorship, vendor relationship, paid contract
- **FORMAL PROFESSIONAL** — employee, officer, attorney, board member, joint venture, co-produced series
- **COMMERCIAL PROMOTION** — directly markets the other's paid product/service
- **RECURRING MEDIA** — repeated interviews/guest appearances
- **SINGLE MEDIA** — one documented appearance
- **CONFERENCE** — same event/program
- **IDEOLOGICAL/TOPICAL** — similar themes only; weakest relationship and never evidence of coordination

Do not describe a media relationship as a financial relationship without proof.

## 5. Money claims

Any revenue, net worth, asset, subscription-income, donation, investment, or money-flow figure requires a source that actually supports that number.

Examples such as "$50 million from Solari" remain **UNVERIFIED CLAIMS** until documentary evidence establishes:
- what entity held/received the money;
- gross vs net;
- relevant date;
- ownership/control;
- source of funds.

## 6. Prediction audit

Predictions must be captured **before** the outcome whenever possible using:
- archived webpages;
- publication dates;
- videos with reliable upload dates;
- newsletters;
- transcripts.

Record exact language. Classify:
- correct;
- partly correct;
- incorrect;
- too vague to score;
- post-hoc / retrospective claim of prediction.

Do not let later reinterpretation replace the original wording.

## 7. Products and deliverables

For every commercial product record:
- seller;
- exact product;
- price / historical price;
- date observed;
- advertised promise;
- actual deliverable;
- refund terms;
- upsells;
- scientific/financial claims;
- independent validation;
- complaints;
- affiliate/referral relationship if documented.

Products include subscriptions, reports, conferences, forecasting software, precious metals, preparedness products, health products, devices, books, courses, memberships, consulting, sponsorships, and referral offers.

## 8. Negative reviews and community reports

Actively search Reddit, forums, BBB, Trustpilot, complaint sites, archived message boards, YouTube discussions, Substack comments, X/Twitter, and other public communities.

Record:
- date;
- username if public;
- exact product/service discussed;
- allegation;
- whether supporting evidence was supplied;
- whether other independent users report the same problem.

Never convert anonymous allegations into verified fact.

## 9. Corrections and defenses

For every major criticism, search for the subject's strongest response. Record it and test it against primary evidence.

This is not "both-sides" padding; it prevents an easy rebuttal that the investigation omitted known contrary facts.

## 10. Writing rule

Final deliverables use plain English.

Preferred structure:

> **What they said:** …
>
> **What the records show:** …
>
> **Why it matters:** …

Avoid legal, accounting, medical, or financial jargon unless immediately explained.

## 11. Evidence retention

Where lawful and practical, preserve:
- canonical URL;
- archive URL;
- publication date;
- access date;
- title;
- author;
- relevant quote or paraphrase;
- screenshot/file reference if needed;
- SHA/hash for locally preserved documents.

Never alter archived source material.

## 12. Publication bar

A damaging assertion should not be published as fact unless supported by reliable evidence proportionate to its seriousness.

The goal is simple:

**Don't protect them. Don't smear them. Document them.**
