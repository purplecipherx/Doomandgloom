# Fear-to-Commerce / Monetization Funnel Audit

## Research question

Does the ecosystem repeatedly convert fear, catastrophe, secrecy, distrust, or extraordinary claims into commercial transactions?

Test the sequence:

**fear / threat claim → urgency or exclusivity → call to action → paid product or service → identifiable beneficiary**

This is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion to assume.

## Capture the sequence, not just the ad

For every relevant video, podcast, newsletter, article, conference, or social post, record:

1. What threat or extraordinary claim is presented?
2. How strongly is it stated?
3. Is evidence supplied?
4. Does the content create urgency?
5. What product/service is promoted nearby?
6. How soon after the fear claim does the pitch occur?
7. What exact call to action is used?
8. What does the product cost?
9. Is there a discount code, deadline, scarcity claim, or act-now message?
10. Who sells the product?
11. Who owns the seller?
12. Does the speaker or host have a documented financial interest?
13. Is an affiliate or sponsorship disclosure present?
14. What evidence supports the product's claims?

## Themes to tag

Banking collapse; currency collapse; CBDC/financial control; confiscation; inflation/hyperinflation; sovereign debt crisis; war/nuclear war; government surveillance; food shortages; grid collapse; cyberattack; pandemic/health emergency; vaccine or medical threat; EMF/radiation; environmental toxin; suppressed technology; secret government program; elite conspiracy; digital identity/social credit; censorship; supply-chain failure; imminent market crash.

## Commercial response categories

Precious metals; paid forecasts; premium reports; newsletters/subscriptions; forecasting software; conferences; preparedness gear; food/storage; supplements; health treatments; diagnostic/biotech devices; EMF/frequency/shielding devices; privacy/cybersecurity products; books/films/courses; consulting; donations/memberships.

## Strong evidence of a fear-to-commerce funnel

A strong case has a dated fear/threat statement, a product pitch in the same or immediately linked content, clear seller/product identity, price, a documented ownership/referral/sponsorship connection, and repeated examples over time.

Especially important: recurring fear narratives before product launches, escalating urgency, recurring sponsor/affiliate relationships, expensive upsells, and weak independent validation for the advertised product.

## Weak evidence

Do not overstate cases where an unrelated ad happens to appear, the host has no documented financial relationship, the product is not positioned as a solution to the threat, or the commercial link is speculative.

## Internal fear-linked monetization score

Score 0-2 each for threat intensity, urgency, product adjacency, financial linkage, claim weakness, and price pressure. Maximum 12. This is for research prioritization only, not proof of deception.

## Upsell ladder audit

Map whether users are moved through:

**free fear content → low-cost product → subscription → premium report → conference/VIP → high-ticket service/product**

Record every step, price, recurring status, seller, ownership, cross-promotion, refund terms, value delivered, and customer complaints.

## Language audit

Preserve exact marketing phrases such as before it's too late, they don't want you to know, protect your family, get out now, last chance, collapse, reset, control grid, survive, secret, exclusive, proprietary, insider, and limited.

Do not infer intent from wording alone. Use the words to document marketing structure.

## Final plain-English output

For each strong example report:

**What scared the audience:** the claim.

**What they were then asked to buy:** product and price.

**Who owned or sold it:** entity.

**How the promoter was connected:** owner, affiliate, sponsor, or unknown.

**Does the product work as advertised?** evidence-based assessment.

**Why this matters:** plain-English explanation.

## Core standard

Build the record so the reader can see exactly **what was said, what was sold, who got paid, and whether the thing sold actually did what was promised.**
