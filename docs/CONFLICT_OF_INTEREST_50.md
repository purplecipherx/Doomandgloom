# 50 High-Value Conflict-of-Interest Tests

These are **investigative hypotheses**, not accusations. Each one becomes a finding only when evidence supports it.

## Ownership, products, and supply chain

1. **Undisclosed product ownership** — a personality recommends a product they directly own.
2. **Indirect ownership through an LLC** — the promoted seller is owned through another company controlled by the promoter.
3. **Family-member ownership** — spouse, child, sibling, or close relative owns the promoted company.
4. **Business-partner ownership** — a recurring collaborator owns the promoted product or seller.
5. **Equity stake in promoted company** — promoter owns shares/options/private equity in the seller.
6. **White-label relationship** — "independent" product is an OEM/white-label item supplied by a connected company.
7. **Connected manufacturer** — another member of the media/network ecosystem manufactures the product.
8. **Connected importer/distributor** — a network member imports or distributes the product while another promotes it.
9. **Licensing/IP relationship** — promoter receives royalties or licensing fees from the promoted product.
10. **Misleading independence** — product is presented as third-party when ownership/control traces back to the same network.

## Affiliate, advertising, sponsorship, and lead generation

11. **Undisclosed affiliate commission** — tracked link/code generates promoter compensation.
12. **Referral fee** — promoter receives payment for sending customers to another business.
13. **Paid sponsorship not clearly disclosed** — company pays for coverage/interview/product mention.
14. **Native ad presented as editorial content** — paid promotion resembles independent analysis.
15. **Conference sponsor conflict** — speaker praises a vendor that financially sponsors the event.
16. **Vendor/exhibitor relationship** — product seller pays for access to the same audience the speaker influences.
17. **Reciprocal promotion** — two businesses repeatedly advertise one another's products.
18. **Cross-affiliate loop** — several network members earn commissions from the same merchants.
19. **Lead-list monetization** — email/member/customer data is sold, rented, or shared with commercial partners.
20. **Paid guest placement** — guest or guest's company pays for an appearance presented as editorial.

## Subscriptions, reports, conferences, and information products

21. **Fear-to-subscription funnel** — alarming claims immediately direct viewers to paid membership.
22. **Fear-to-report funnel** — imminent crisis rhetoric sells a high-priced special report.
23. **Free-to-premium upsell ladder** — free content intentionally routes users into progressively higher-priced tiers.
24. **Prediction conflict** — person selling forecasts publicly promotes claims that increase demand for the forecast service.
25. **Conference self-dealing** — organizer, speaker, vendor, and product seller are financially connected.
26. **Paid speaking conflict** — speaker praises or validates the organization paying the speaking fee.
27. **Premium access conflict** — "exclusive information" claim is used to justify high-ticket membership despite public availability.
28. **Book/documentary sales conflict** — sensational claim simultaneously markets a book, film, course, or documentary.
29. **Donation-to-access model** — donations or memberships unlock influence, visibility, introductions, or premium content.
30. **Refund/cancellation friction as revenue strategy** — recurring revenue depends materially on difficult cancellation or restrictive refunds.

## Securities, commodities, crypto, and investment conflicts

31. **Short-and-distort risk** — bearish public claims while holding short stock, puts, swaps, or other downside exposure.
32. **Pump-and-dump risk** — bullish promotion while holding a position intended for sale into increased demand.
33. **Front-running own audience** — position is opened before publishing a market-moving recommendation.
34. **Trading after private access** — trade occurs after receiving nonpublic information through interviews, clients, conferences, or insiders.
35. **Commodity inventory conflict** — person predicts scarcity/rising prices while selling or owning inventory of the commodity.
36. **Precious-metals dealer conflict** — collapse/inflation messaging directly benefits a connected metals dealer.
37. **Crypto/token allocation conflict** — promoter received tokens, warrants, advisory allocation, or discounted presale access.
38. **Issuer-funded research** — company being analyzed pays the analyst/newsletter/researcher.
39. **Competitor-funded attack** — negative coverage is funded by a competitor or investor who benefits from decline.
40. **Adviser/fund position conflict** — registered adviser, fund, family office, or connected investment vehicle holds the discussed security.

## Research, health, science, and expert conflicts

41. **Study funded by advocate** — research supporting a claim is funded by the person/org promoting that claim.
42. **Researcher owns relevant patent/IP** — expert validating a product can profit from adoption.
43. **Manufacturer-funded validation** — product's "independent study" was funded or controlled by manufacturer.
44. **Ghostwritten or commissioned research** — nominally independent paper/report was written or shaped by interested party.
45. **Expert-witness/consulting compensation** — expert making public claims is simultaneously paid by interested litigant/company.
46. **Medical-product sales conflict** — clinician/expert recommends supplement/device/treatment they sell or receive commissions from.
47. **Nonprofit-to-for-profit related-party payments** — nonprofit pays a company controlled by insiders or close associates.
48. **Grant/donation reciprocity** — organization funds researcher/media outlet that later promotes the funder's claims/products.
49. **Shared legal/PR/marketing infrastructure** — supposedly independent entities use the same paid strategist, agency, counsel, or campaign infrastructure in ways relevant to promotion.
50. **Hidden beneficial owner / shell-company routing** — money or ownership passes through entities that obscure who ultimately benefits.

## Required evidence fields for every suspected conflict

- person/entity
- product/security/claim involved
- exact public statement
- date/time
- money/ownership relationship
- disclosure made to audience?
- amount or rate if known
- primary documentary evidence
- independent corroboration
- alternative explanation
- assessment: VERIFIED / SUPPORTED / SUSPECTED / UNVERIFIED / CONTRADICTED
- confidence
- source IDs

## High-value principle

The strongest conflict-of-interest finding is not:
> "These people know each other."

It is:
> "Person A made Claim X, promoted Product Y as the solution, had Financial Interest Z in Y, and did not disclose Z in the content where the recommendation was made."

That is the standard to aim for.
