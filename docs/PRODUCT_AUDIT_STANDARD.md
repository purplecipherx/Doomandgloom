# Product / Offer Audit Standard

Every commercial offer discovered in the Doomandgloom ecosystem is treated as an investigative object in its own right.

## 1. Products include more than physical goods

Track all of the following:

- paid reports and newsletters
- subscriptions and memberships
- forecasting software / proprietary models / trading systems
- conferences, VIP passes, consultations, courses, seminars
- books, films, DVDs, downloadable research
- precious-metals offers, storage programs, referral offers
- preparedness products
- supplements and health products
- diagnostic / therapeutic / wellness devices
- EMF / shielding / frequency products
- "DNA", biotechnology, sequencing, gene, cellular, plasma, Rife, bioresonance, or similar products
- software, data products, apps, dashboards, investment services
- affiliate products promoted by members of the network
- products sold by sponsors of recurring shows/events

## 2. Ownership must be traced

For each product identify, where public evidence permits:

1. Brand name
2. Actual seller / merchant of record
3. Manufacturer
4. Legal entity that owns the brand
5. Beneficial owner(s), officers, directors, or principals
6. Whether a person/entity in the research network owns or controls it
7. Whether a network member receives:
   - affiliate commission
   - referral fee
   - sponsorship money
   - reseller margin
   - licensing income
   - speaker/vendor fee
   - equity/ownership benefit

An interview, endorsement, or shared conference appearance is **not** evidence of ownership or payment.

## 3. Marketing-claim audit

Capture the exact marketing claims, especially:

- guaranteed or unusually strong investment performance
- proprietary predictive accuracy
- medical/diagnostic benefit
- disease treatment/prevention
- DNA/genetic effects
- detoxification
- radiation/EMF protection
- frequency/energy claims
- "military" / "government" technology claims
- scarcity / suppressed-technology claims
- "they don't want you to know" claims
- survival/collapse urgency
- promises of exclusive/inside information

For each claim record:

**What is promised → what evidence is supplied → what independent evidence exists → what regulators/science say → assessment**

## 4. Validity classifications

Use only evidence-backed classifications:

- **SUPPORTED** — strong independent evidence supports the central claim
- **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** — some claimed function is real, but marketing overstates it
- **UNVALIDATED** — insufficient independent evidence
- **CONTRADICTED** — reliable evidence conflicts with the advertised claim
- **MISLEADING MARKETING** — technically true fragments presented in a way likely to create a materially false impression
- **REGULATORY CONCERN** — warning letter, enforcement, recall, unapproved medical claims, registration problem, etc.
- **FRAUDULENT / FAKE** — use only when supported by an adjudication, regulator finding, demonstrable counterfeit/fabrication, or equivalently strong evidence

Do **not** label something fake merely because it sounds implausible.

## 5. Quality / "subpar" testing

A product can be real yet poor value or technically inferior.

Assess "subpar" against an explicit comparator:

- price versus normal market alternatives
- specifications versus comparable products
- accuracy versus benchmark
- published predictive record versus baseline
- ingredients/dose versus accepted standards
- independent laboratory testing
- warranty/refund terms
- software reliability
- customer support
- completeness of promised deliverables

Classify quality:

- **ABOVE COMPARATOR**
- **COMPETITIVE**
- **BELOW COMPARATOR**
- **POOR VALUE**
- **INSUFFICIENT DATA**

Never call something "subpar" without saying **subpar compared with what**.

## 6. Health / biotech / device validation

For any health, DNA, medical, therapeutic, diagnostic, EMF, frequency, or biotechnology product, specifically search:

- FDA databases and warning letters
- FTC enforcement
- ClinicalTrials.gov where relevant
- PubMed / peer-reviewed literature
- device clearance/approval numbers
- manufacturer registrations
- patents (not evidence that a product works)
- independent lab validation
- adverse-event / recall information where applicable

Distinguish:
- FDA registered
- FDA listed
- FDA cleared
- FDA authorized
- FDA approved

These are not interchangeable.

## 7. Financial product validation

For forecasts, reports, investment systems, and trading tools:

- archive predictions before the outcome
- measure hit/miss rate where feasible
- compare with simple benchmarks
- distinguish vague predictions from falsifiable ones
- identify deleted/changed/post-hoc predictions
- document subscription/report price and upsells
- look for SEC/CFTC/FINRA/regulatory history when relevant

A handful of highlighted wins is not a valid performance record.

## 8. Review / complaint evidence

Collect negative and positive reviews, but separate:

- verified transactions
- named customers
- anonymous posts
- affiliate reviewers
- sponsored testimonials
- company-selected testimonials
- regulator/court complaints

Look for recurring complaint themes across independent platforms.

Customer comments support the statement:
> "Customers have reported X."

They do not, by themselves, establish:
> "X definitely happened."

## 9. Product-to-network web

Products are graph nodes.

Create edges for:
- OWNS
- MANUFACTURES
- SELLS
- PROMOTES
- AFFILIATE_REFERRAL
- SPONSORS
- FEATURED_AT_CONFERENCE
- BUNDLED_WITH
- LICENSES
- INVESTS_IN

This allows us to identify whether an apparently independent media personality repeatedly promotes products owned by another member of the same ecosystem.

## 10. Deliverable fields

Every product's final plain-English entry should answer:

**What is it?**
**Who actually owns/makes it?**
**Who in the network promotes it?**
**How much does it cost?**
**What does the advertising promise?**
**What do you actually receive?**
**Is the central claim independently validated?**
**Is there regulatory history?**
**What do dissatisfied customers consistently report?**
**Is it materially worse or more expensive than ordinary alternatives?**
**Does money appear to flow back to anyone else in the network?**
**What remains unknown?**
